Ranking and Fusion Strategies

Lexical retrieval compares the words shared between a query and a document.
TF-IDF weights each term by how often it appears in one document against how
rare it is across the collection. It excels at exact matches such as product
codes, proper nouns and acronyms, but it cannot connect synonyms.

Dense retrieval encodes text as vectors produced by a neural network, so that
passages with similar meaning end up close together in the vector space. This
finds a passage about a feline when the question mentions a cat, even though the
two words never coincide.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion merges several ranked lists without comparing their
scores, which are not on the same scale. Each document receives the sum of one
divided by a constant plus its position in every list. A passage ranked highly by
both strategies rises to the top, while a passage found by only one still makes
it into the results.

Cross-encoder reranking is a second pass over a small candidate set. Instead of
encoding query and passage separately, the model reads both together and scores
their relevance directly, which is far more accurate but too slow to run over an
entire collection.
