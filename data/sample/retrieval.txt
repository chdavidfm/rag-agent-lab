Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly abbreviated as RAG, is a technique that
combines two steps. First, the system retrieves the passages most relevant to a
question from a document collection. Then a language model writes an answer
grounded exclusively in those passages.

The main benefit is factual grounding. A language model on its own may fabricate
plausible but false statements, a failure mode known as hallucination. When the
model is handed verified passages, its answer can be traced back to a source.

Updating the knowledge of a RAG system requires no retraining at all. Replacing
the underlying documents is enough, which makes the approach far cheaper to
maintain than fine-tuning a model every time the information changes.

A basic RAG pipeline has four stages: chunking the documents into passages,
indexing those passages as numerical vectors, retrieving the closest ones to the
question, and generating the final answer from them.
